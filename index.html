The principle

AI in support should do the repetitive work so humans stay the warm, accountable element in the relationship.

Anyone can paste a ticket into a chatbot. The scarce skill is designing the prompt, the verification step, and the handoff so the human stays accountable for what goes into the CRM and out to the customer.

Most customers don’t call with a part number. They call with what it’s for, how they use it, or the item they bought years ago. I start with discovery questions before I offer a fix — those answers are what get us to the right item, not the first guess. I’m at my best when the case is hard, and my goal is the work that prevents repeats: customer experience operations.

That through-line runs through my whole career. I started in 911 dispatch, triaging emergencies under pressure, then carried that composure into insurance claims and mortgage processing — investigating files, enforcing standards, and coordinating across underwriters, closers, realtors, and vendors. I proved I could run operations when I lifted property occupancy from 78% to 96%, then retooled for tech: a software engineering fellowship, an open-source fellowship at Sauce Labs, and hands-on AI workflow design for support.

At Zoro, working complex cases has clarified what I want next: the systems side of customer experience, where practical AI — on company-approved tools only — and investigation habits prevent repeat issues instead of only resolving them one at a time. Personal AI agent projects stay outside of work.

Workflow design

Prompt libraries & verification habits

Reusable prompts for ownership-driven customer emails and structured documentation. I emphasize checking sources, verifying item numbers, and clearly labeling what cannot be confirmed before anything reaches a customer or the CRM.

Human-reviewed by design

Method

Search Gap — from vague request to the right item

When the customer’s language doesn’t match the catalog, I start with their exact words, use company-approved Gemini to clarify search terms, match on function, size, and use (not a guessed SKU name), find a suitable alternative when there’s no exact match, and verify item number, specs, and fit before anything reaches the customer.

Human-reviewed method · company-approved tools only
